How kb_search finds an answer

Every search goes through two rankings run in parallel, a merge step and a local "judge" model that re-reads the best candidates. At the end the judge says how confident it is that the answer is really in the knowledge base.

Why each stage is there

Each stage fixes a specific weakness of the one before it.

Semantic search

Turns the question and every chunk into vectors and compares their meaning. It finds paraphrases with no words in common.

Weak spot: it blurs exact identifiers such as table names, ports and hostnames.

"worker stuck" ≈ "queue not draining"

BM25

Classic keyword scoring: a chunk ranks high if it contains the query's words, especially rare ones.

Weak spot: no words in common means no match.

"orders_db" → the one chunk that says orders_db

RRF merge

Reciprocal Rank Fusion merges the two lists by position, not score, because the two scales can't be compared: Σ 1/(60 + rank).

A chunk near the top of either list survives.

top 10 kept for the next stage

Reranker

A small cross-encoder reads each (question, chunk) pair together and scores how well the chunk answers it. It generates no text, which is the same idea as a "System One" model like Jev.

It runs locally, so no chunk leaves the machine.

Calibrated confidence

The best reranker score becomes a probability via Platt scaling: p = σ(0.392·s + 0.356). "0.8" means right about 80% of the time.

Below 0.5, kb_search warns that the answer is probably not in the KB.

fitted with 5-fold cross-validation

Where the time goes

One search, end to end, on CPU. The reranker is almost all of it, and it is what buys the precision.

The first two segments are drawn wider than their real share (~15 ms together) so they stay readable. Before: ~15 ms, but the right section was at rank 1 only 64% of the time and there was no way to say "not found". KB_RERANK=off brings the fast version back.

Honest limits

57 questions is a small sample. The numbers are good evidence, not a guarantee. In the confidence chart, three questions (the red dots on the right) scored high even though the right section wasn't in the top 5: the model can still be confidently wrong, just much more rarely than before. The 7 questions with no answer all ended up below the warning threshold.
